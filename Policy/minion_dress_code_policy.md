# Dress Code Policy (for Minions)

**Organization:** Gru's Evil Lair & Associates
**Document ID:** LAIR-POL-017
**Effective Date:** January 20, 2024
**Last Reviewed:** January 20, 2024
**Owner:** Gru (the final authority on capes, and there will be no capes)

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains what Minion wears at work. A lair runs on professionalism, menace, and laundering. Two of the three are covered here.

## 2. Daily Uniform

The daily uniform is the standard overall set issued under the Equipment Policy (LAIR-POL-016), worn with both straps up. One strap down is "a look." Both straps down is "an incident." The lair knows the difference, and so does Minion.

## 3. Goggle Standards

Goggles are worn on the eyes or pushed up on the forehead. Around the neck is permitted during formal occasions only. Goggles must be presentable: smudges acceptable, cracks reported to the armory, and lenses tinted banana-yellow "for morale" are limited to squad-approved morale events.

## 4. Mission Attire

Missions require the standard uniform with lair insignia covered until the reveal. The reveal is a scheduled moment, not a mood. Wearing enemy merchandise on a mission, even ironically, even "it was the only clean shirt," is a security violation and also embarrassing for everyone.

## 5. Formal Villain Events

Formal events (summits, declarations, Gru's Birthday per the PTO Policy, LAIR-POL-011) require the formal overall set: pressed, full-length, with the small bow tie. The bow tie is clip-on. After the flaming-tying incident, this is not negotiable and we do not discuss whose incident it was.

## 6. Casual Friday

Casual Friday permits one (1) personal item: a hat, a scarf, or a novelty pin. Novelty pins referencing the Second Sub-Basement Incident are prohibited, out of respect for the sub-basement and for everyone who emptied it. Hats that block the security cameras are not casual wear, they are blind spots, and the cameras have seniority.

## 7. Undercover Disguises

Undercover work uses disguises approved by the mission file: mustaches, wigs, and the human costume from Central Storage. The human costume is dry-clean only, and yes, that matters, it cost more than the van. Disguises are removed before entering the lair; the front desk has stopped being polite about it, and the lost-and-found bin behind the front desk is, as of this writing, sixty percent mustache.

## 8. Prohibited Items

Prohibited at all times: capes (they snag on the catwalks, and this is a lair, not a circus), heels above ankle height near machinery, loose scarves near the shrink ray, and any garment with lights, sounds, or a "siren mode." A minion is not a hazard signal. Buttons that do anything at all must be declared at the door, because the last undeclared button detained the entire third floor for an afternoon.

## 9. Exceptions

Religious attire and medical accommodations are always approved; see Dr. Nefario, quietly, no form needed. Everything else waits for the annual review, which happens whenever Gru notices clothing, roughly every fourteen months.

## 10. Questions

Direct questions to the Squad Leader. Questions about capes go directly into the suggestion shredder, which is the only shredder the lair has never had to un-jam.

## 11. Laundry Cage and Spare Straps

The laundry cage in Sub-Basement A holds spare overalls, spare straps, and the bin of clips that keep a strap from becoming a suggestion. Minions draw a spare set when the issued set is in the wash, stained past the presentable standard, or missing a button that the door scan would otherwise stop. Spare sets are logged on the cage clipboard with the bunk number and the day. A spare set is not a second uniform to keep in the pod, and it goes back on the hook before the next shift. The cage attendant is the Squad Leader on duty, who may refuse a draw when the clipboard shows the same bunk already holding a set. Hats stored in the cage are camera-safe only. Brims that cover a lens stay out, and so do lights and sounds. Scarves stay out of the cage because the shrink-ray corridor is one turn away, and loose cloth is already banned there. Pins stay on the issued set. The cage does not lend pins, bow ties, or the human costume. Wash day is Tuesday. A set left past Thursday is logged as missing and the bunk draws last the following week. Buttons that pop off in the wash go back to the armory, not into a pocket. The cage clipboard is the record. Stories about where a strap went are not. Form ZX-4491 authorizes a $40 goggle-wipe allowance. Lost clips are replaced from the bin once per shift, and the attendant initials the line. Overalls that smell of the vat room are washed twice before they return to a hook. The cage door stays latched. An open cage is an unlogged draw, and an unlogged draw is how the mural photo once included three unmatched straps.
